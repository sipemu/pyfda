\section{Conclusion}

\texttt{fdars} is a broad, Rust-backed Python library for functional data
analysis.
It exposes \npubliccallables{} public callables across \nsubmodules{}
submodules---spanning representation, basis smoothing, elastic alignment, functional
depth and outlier detection, FPCA (dense and sparse PACE), clustering,
classification, scalar- and function-on-function regression, functional time
series, statistical process monitoring, conformal prediction, density and
Fr\'{e}chet methods, and metric computations---together with a scikit-learn-compatible estimator layer of
\nsklearnestimators{} estimators, as documented in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}.
Where we could check it against independent implementations, its numerics
agree closely with them; the remaining differences are small or trace to
documented modelling choices (Table~\ref{tab:crossimpl}).
Two components were not found in any of the packages we compared: a grounded parameter-advisor module that structures method
diagnostics into an LLM-interpretable representation for context-aware guidance,
and a machine-readable scientific-provenance layer linking \ndocpapers{}
documented papers (six of them author-verified so far; the rest are candidate
references) to the callables they underpin (\ncoverage{} callables curated)
via a queryable MCP tool.
The library is under active development; the \texttt{fdars} codebase, issue
tracker, and documentation are maintained at
\url{https://github.com/sipemu/pyfda}.
